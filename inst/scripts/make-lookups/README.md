# make-lookups: the three catalogue lookups

`lookup_blood_hg19`, `lookup_nasal_epithelium_hg19` and
`lookup_solid_tissue_hg19` pair every EPIC v2 CpG with candidate target genes.
For each pair they give the direction predicted by a tissue-specific model of
measured eQTMs: a `PREDICTED` call or an `ABSTAIN`. Measured pairs of a
training cohort that fall inside the candidate window are `DIRECT_eQTM`, with
the measured sign.

Since cpgdirectionData 0.99.7 (8-9 November 2026) the whole chain is here, from
the raw eQTM sources to the training pools, the models and the lookups. It is
deterministic: a rerun reproduces the models and the three Hub files byte for
byte, and every table's content (their gzip headers carry a timestamp).

## Why the chain was rebuilt (0.99.7)

0.99.5 corrected the gene placement of the lookups. It kept the original
training pools and the saved models (`per_tissue_models.pkl`), whose build
was never committed. Those pools and models had five problems:

1. **Wrong-end distances (blood).** Framingham's TSS is the gene's leftmost
   coordinate. For minus-strand genes the training distances therefore ran
   to the 3' end: 2,654 rows (5.9% of the pool), measured from a point a
   median 15.8 kb from the Ensembl TSS.
2. **Non-hg19 TSSs (solid).** TCGA distances took the first Atlas row's TSS,
   which is not hg19 for genes first seen in Framingham or GTEx: 156 rows
   (1.3%), a median of about 394 kb off.
3. **One gene, several identities.** The cohorts name genes differently: GTEx
   ENSG IDs, Framingham clone names, EVA-PR symbols, and renamed HGNC symbols
   such as MLLT4 and AFDN. The pools, the gene-prior feature and the lookups
   treated each name as its own gene, so one gene could take two of a CpG's
   seven candidate slots.
4. **A pseudo-gene (blood).** 3,935 HELIX rows from 1,461 transcript clusters
   with no gene name were one gene, "NAN".
5. **Not reproducible, and an inconsistent fit.**
   - The saved calibrators and the solid model cannot be regenerated by any
     procedure that can be recovered.
   - The original procedure also fitted each calibrator to models unlike the
     final one. scikit-learn's early stopping, on above 10,000 rows, left the
     solid fold models at 400 iterations but stopped the final solid model
     early (174 iterations).
   - Every final model left 10% of its pool out.

## The pipeline

| step | script | does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `export_inputs.R` | writes the Ensembl GRCh37 gene table (annotables `grch37`) and the HGNC symbol table (HGNChelper) |
| 2 | `build_pools.py` | one gene identity, one TSS, harmonized training pools |
| 3 | `train_models.py` | fits, calibrates and saves the three models |
| 4 | `build_lookups.py` | the three lookups |
| 5 | `write_rds.R` | the Hub resources |

The rules are in each script's header. In short:

- **Identity.** Every pool name is resolved to one Ensembl GRCh37 gene, on
  the chromosome of its own pool CpGs. Names are matched by ENSG ID, by
  symbol, or by HGNC previous or alias symbol. A match is accepted when the
  cohort's own hg19 coordinate for the gene lies within the gene ± 10 kb. If
  several genes are accepted, the one whose TSS lies within 10 bp of that
  coordinate is taken. Otherwise, in this order:
  1. for a symbol that Ensembl carries under several IDs, the lowest ID (a
     deterministic tie-break);
  2. an exact, non-clone symbol whose coordinate lies within 100 kb of the
     gene: an upstream first exon that Ensembl GRCh37 lacks, as for TLR2;
  3. the one Ensembl gene whose TSS lies within 10 bp of that coordinate;
  4. else the name is dropped.

  Of the 11,478 pool names:
  - 11,005 resolve to 10,725 genes, and 267 genes carry more than one name;
  - by rule: 9,956 by symbol, 311 by HGNC previous/alias symbol, 78 by ENSG
    ID, 602 by position, 30 as the lowest of several IDs and 28 at up to
    100 kb;
  - 473 are not identified.

  HELIX rows whose name is not identified (blank, multi-gene or unknown
  names) keep their transcript cluster as their identity. They train the
  model but are never candidates.
- **TSS.** Each gene has one 1-based, strand-aware TSS. It is the cohort's
  own hg19 TSS where that coordinate agrees with the gene's identity (on its
  chromosome, within the window the identity accepted); otherwise it is the
  Ensembl GRCh37 gene TSS. The cohort TSS is the EVA-PR first-row TSS (+1 on
  the plus strand, where EVA-PR is 0-based), else the HELIX TSS.
  - Sources: EVA-PR 9,669 genes, HELIX 679, Ensembl 377.
  - The training distances and the lookups use the same TSS: `dist` = (CpG
    position − TSS) × strand.
- **Display name.** A gene is shown under a cohort's own name, preferring
  EVA-PR's for continuity with earlier lookups. A name identified by
  position that is, or contains, another Ensembl gene's symbol is never used
  (for example, HELIX's "IFI6" at TRBV6-1); with no name left, the Ensembl
  symbol is shown.
- **Pools.** These are the original cohort × CpG × gene rows and labels,
  harmonized:
  - rows of unidentified names, of genes on another chromosome, or more than
    1 Mb from the TSS are dropped;
  - within a cohort, two rows of one CpG and one gene are one row when their
    labels agree, and are dropped when they disagree.
- **Models.** The original procedure (`eqtm-direction/analysis/04`), with the
  gene prior keyed by gene ID:
  - `lib_eqtm` features, HistGradientBoosting with seed 0;
  - 5-fold GroupKFold by CpG for out-of-fold predictions, and an isotonic
    calibrator fitted on them;
  - the model refitted on the whole pool;
  - leave-one-cohort-out AUCs reported.

  One change: early stopping is off, so every fit runs 400 iterations on all
  its rows and the calibrator matches the model that ships.
- **Lookups.** The candidate rule, tiers, call thresholds and `DIRECT_eQTM`
  rule are those of 0.99.5:
  - a gene's names are one candidate;
  - `DIRECT_eQTM` pairs are matched by gene ID;
  - every candidate is a pool gene, so tier B means "no catalogued CpG within
    50 kb" and tier C does not occur.

  One tissue-specific rule (PI ruling): the solid model's tier-B calls
  abstain. Against the solid yardsticks they agree 0.41 (123 pairs, 95% CI
  0.32-0.49), below chance.

### Validation

The lookups were checked against yardsticks the models never saw:
- MESA, for adult blood;
- HELIX Dryad pairs outside the blood catalogue, for child blood;
- EVA-PR nasal pairs outside the nasal catalogue;
- FUSION and GTEx solid.

Genes were matched by Ensembl ID, and each fold used one fixed set of pairs.
The comparison is with 0.99.5, at ladder level, with 95% CIs.

- **Blood and nasal hold.**
  - MESA −0.008 (−0.017 to −0.000), HELIX +0.011, EVA-PR nasal +0.036 (49
    to 86 pairs): differences the size refitting alone produces.
  - The blood model agrees 0.717 with MESA (0.712 before).
- **Where a tissue model calls a yardstick pair in both builds, 7 of 14,670
  signs differ.**
- **Solid is lower.**
  - FUSION −0.001; GTEx solid −0.021 (−0.034 to −0.009).
  - The solid model's tier-A calls agree 0.644 with FUSION and 0.618 with
    GTEx solid (0.99.5: 0.677 and 0.674).
- **The solid model is unstable.** Over 11 retrainings its tier-A agreement
  ranged 0.60-0.69 with FUSION and 0.62-0.68 with GTEx solid. The retrainings
  varied the seed, the fold assignment, early stopping, the iteration count
  and bagging, on pools that differ by 0.4% of rows. The ladder quotes each
  source's validated figure, so solid calls carry the lower number.
- **The Ensembl-TSS variant is worse.** It put every gene at the Ensembl gene
  TSS (`build_pools.py --tss ensembl`) and was compared like for like, under
  the identity rules of the time. It was significantly worse on FUSION
  (−0.006) and GTEx solid (−0.019), where the cohort-TSS build was not
  (−0.003, −0.010). It did better on MESA and EVA-PR nasal, but not
  significantly. It is not shipped.

## Reproduce

1. **Python environment.** Python 3 with scikit-learn 1.7.2 (the scripts
   refuse any other), pandas 2.3.3 and numpy 2.2.6:
   `python3 -m venv venv && venv/bin/pip install scikit-learn==1.7.2 pandas==2.3.3 numpy==2.2.6`
2. **R environment.** data.table and HGNChelper (0.8.16 was used). You also
   need the annotables `grch37.rda` (md5 fec180c1c9a29646053531e9f729e4a4)
   from
   <https://raw.githubusercontent.com/stephenturner/annotables/master/data/grch37.rda>.
3. **Sources (RAW).**
   - The five eQTM Atlas exports, under their original file names:
     `EVA-PR_ All Subjects_data.csv`,
     `EVA-PR_ Atopic asthma + controls_data.csv`,
     `EVA-PR_ White Blood Cell_data.csv`,
     `Framingham Heart Study_data (1).csv` and `GTEx_ WholeBlood_data.csv`.
     They come from <https://shiny.crc.pitt.edu/eqtm_browser/> (Downloads
     tab).
   - The HELIX catalogue `eQTM_autosome_adj.cells_SIG.txt.gz` (Ruiz-Arenas et
     al. 2022).
   - The five `EWAS_OpenEWAS_EPICv1_block_*` folders (TCGA via OpenEWAS).
   - `ann_hg19.bed`, the EPIC v2 manifest lifted to hg19.
4. **Commands** (in this folder):

```
Rscript export_inputs.R inputs grch37.rda
python3 build_pools.py --raw RAW --inputs inputs --out pools
python3 train_models.py --pools pools --out models.pkl --report pools/train_report.csv
python3 build_lookups.py --pools pools --models models.pkl --raw RAW --out out
Rscript write_rds.R out hub_upload <previous hub_upload>
```

`lib_eqtm.py` is a verbatim copy of `eqtm-direction/analysis/lib_eqtm.py`
(md5 0defbd144a6b13995d1fdd296d7638b6). A full run takes about 10 minutes.
